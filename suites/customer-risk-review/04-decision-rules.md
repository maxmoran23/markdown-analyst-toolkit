# Decision rules — Customer Risk Review

## Rules for the illustrative index

| Condition | Required analytical result | Prohibited shortcut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Five values present, each 0–3, weights sum to 1 | Calculate unrounded contributions and score | Treat score as likelihood of crime |
| Any value absent | Composite null; band INCOMPLETE | Replace missing with zero or reweight |
| Value outside scale | Reject affected computation pending correction | Clamp silently to maximum |
| Duplicate customer-factor pair | Resolve version or duplication first | Average conflicting rows |
| Different factor framework | State its provenance and rules explicitly | Blend weights from unrelated methods |
| Material unresolved fact | Add targeted review regardless of score | Treat LOW as approval |
| Claimed control without operating evidence | Preserve claim and limitation | Apply a numerical reduction |
| Human override proposed | Record rationale, authority, evidence, and pending state | Pretend the override was approved |

The illustrative bands route discussion; they do not define a legal duty or universal institutional standard. Boundary values of 35 and 65 belong in MODERATE and HIGH respectively. A score is not a calibrated probability, and a difference of ten points has no established interpretation as a proportionate increase in AML risk. Do not rank people by an apparent decimal precision that the qualitative inputs cannot support.

## Evidence confidence and severity

Use HIGH confidence when the narrow observation is directly reproducible from the supplied evidence with low ambiguity. Use MODERATE when identification, comparability, or interpretation is partly unresolved. Use LOW when a material inference relies on a weak or incomplete basis. A synthetic arithmetic result may have HIGH confidence as an exercise calculation while confidence about any real customer remains inapplicable. Do not use a confident mathematical statement to imply source authenticity.

Severity describes the consequence of the finding for this review. HIGH can describe an unresolved decision-critical gap; it does not allege misconduct. MEDIUM can describe a relevant inconsistency whose effect remains bounded. LOW can describe a documentation issue with no demonstrated effect on the current analytical conclusion. Describe the consequence in words so the label remains challengeable. Confidence and severity are independent: a potentially consequential allegation may have low evidentiary confidence.

## Review priority and human disposition

Use STANDARD for a profile without identified targeted gaps under the stated evidence scope, HEIGHTENED for additional contextual review, TARGETED for a specific material question, SPECIALIST when the issue needs expertise beyond this method, and INCOMPLETE when basic calculations or understanding cannot be finished. These labels are proposed work priorities, not customer ratings. Do not imply that STANDARD means all regulatory obligations were completed.

When a reviewer wants an override, preserve the original computation. Record the proposed replacement conclusion, its basis, why the scoring inputs do not capture the issue, and evidence that could reverse it. The assistant can recommend review but cannot record a person's approval or invent an institution's acceptance standard. Unassigned owners remain role labels. Due dates are proposed dates unless explicitly accepted by an authorized person.

## No automatic relationship outcomes

Do not infer approval, denial, restriction, exit, or a filing obligation from a band, nationality, public-function status, business category, or absence of public information alone. Actual decisions require the applicable rules, permitted evidence, and an authorized process. A separate sanctions or legal question cannot be offset by favorable customer factors. Preserve it as a distinct specialist question rather than burying it in an average.

Where the evidence conflicts, the recommendation should specify what can be concluded now and what remains contingent. “C05 has the lowest illustrative index, and its planned third-party funds remain unexplained” is a coherent result. “C05 is safe” is not supported. If no additional evidence is obtainable, state that limitation and the consequences for the proposed review; do not fill the gap with a narrative that merely sounds plausible.
