# Inputs and evidence — Jurisdiction Risk and Exposure Review

## Geography and exposure input requirements

Begin with the population definition: transfer, customer, counterparty, asset, office, or expected relationship exposure. Capture the relevant role for each geography field. Do not call a mailing address nationality or infer residence from currency. A bank identifier may locate an institution but not the customer, beneficial owner, economic beneficiary, or underlying business activity. Each inferred mapping requires a documented basis and an uncertainty status.

For transfers, retain unique economic-event IDs, posting and value dates, direction, amount, currency, destination role, origin role if supplied, intermediaries, subject linkage, and inclusion/exclusion rationale. Reversals and returns must link to the original event. Decide whether the analysis is gross activity, net settlement, or outstanding exposure before aggregation. A returned transfer can count in a gross activity analysis while netting to zero in a settlement view; the two measures answer different questions.

For customer geography, capture incorporation, principal operations, residence where relevant, ownership/control connections, counterparties, and expected payment corridors separately. Avoid requiring personal information beyond the permitted public or fictional scope. Missing underlying-party geography is not cured by a known intermediary location. A high-quality payment route record can coexist with an unresolved economic origin.

## Jurisdiction context records

Each context item needs issuing authority, document title, exact relevant passage, published date, effective date or period, geographic coverage, definition, source limitation, and review cutoff. Separate international standard-setting statements from legally operative local implementation. For sanctions, include the relevant program, prohibited activity or party category, jurisdictional nexus, applicable date, and any identified exception or authorization question. A country name on a program page does not by itself answer those questions.

Corruption-related research needs its measurement definition, vintage, direction, units, uncertainty, and suitability for the question. A survey-derived governance indicator and an established criminal finding are different kinds of evidence. Do not translate a numerical index into a probability that a particular resident is corrupt. This suite's fictional qualitative descriptors are teaching inputs with no external calibration.

## Public references actually reviewed

- REF-FATF: [FATF monitored-jurisdiction category descriptions](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/countries/black-and-grey-lists.html), introductory definitions for increased monitoring and call for action, reviewed Oct 09, 2026. These are distinct strategic-deficiency processes. The source is dynamic; no membership list is reproduced here. Legal implementation and the exact current statement require separate review.
- REF-OFAC: [OFAC Sanctions Programs and Country Information](https://ofac.treasury.gov/sanctions-programs-and-country-information), introductory program scope, reviewed Oct 09, 2026. OFAC describes comprehensive and selective programs. This supports checking the relevant program and facts; it does not establish a transaction prohibition from a country label.
- REF-WGI: [World Bank Worldwide Governance Indicators](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/worldwide-governance-indicators), “What are the WGI?” and “Usage Advisory,” reviewed Oct 09, 2026. The page identifies perception-based estimates and limits their use as definitive criteria for consequential financial decisions. No real indicator values or jurisdiction rankings are used in the fixture.

The author's retrieval supports only these narrow descriptions. Users must refresh sources for their own dated task. This manual suite cannot watch for list changes, update ratings, screen counterparties, or maintain an operational restrictions inventory.

## Evidence classification and intake ledger

Assign every supplied item a stable evidence ID before drawing a conclusion. Record title, issuer, exact page or table locator, retrieval status, publication date, effective date, review cutoff, subject identifier, and the proposition it can support. These attributes belong in the complete saved case record even where the fixed workbook offers fewer columns. The common Evidence sheet follows 07; do not add invented mandatory columns to its schema. A source title is not proof of retrieval, and a search-result snippet is not a substitute for the operative text.

Use Observed for what is directly visible in a record, Reported for a subject's assertion, Inferred for a conclusion drawn from identified observations, and Estimated for a value derived with stated assumptions. Observing a declaration establishes that the declaration exists. It does not independently establish its truth. The fictional fixture is Observed as supplied exercise data while its purported customer statements remain fictional assertions. Public authority references support methodological context; they do not corroborate fictional subjects.

Maintain a manifest of accepted and rejected inputs. Identify duplicate versions, unreadable pages, omitted periods, contradictory statements, and redactions that affect the task. Retain rejected rows with exclusion reasons; do not silently remove awkward evidence. If an attachment is partially readable, name the readable span and preserve a separate unassessed remainder. A count derived from visible rows must not be labelled the full population unless the manifest reconciles it.

Use only public sources, fictional data, or generic permitted material suitable for this personal analytical workflow. Do not request actual account numbers, identity documents, private employment information, privileged advice, or employer records. An analyst who cannot safely supply a material fact can substitute a clearly fictional value for method testing. That substitution supports the exercise only; it cannot support a real disposition. Never claim a fictitious document was obtained from an authority.

## Dates, comparability, and source conflicts

A document can be authentic yet unsuitable for the decision because its entity, period, purpose, or measurement basis differs. Preserve those dimensions before merging evidence. Record both publication and effective dates where available; later publication does not necessarily supersede an earlier operative event. For a historical review, use evidence effective at the historical cutoff and identify later information separately. Do not backfill current knowledge into the historical conclusion without marking the restatement.

When two sources disagree, present each proposition, the difference, and the decisive additional evidence. Authority hierarchy alone cannot fix an entity mismatch or a transcription error. Seek the relevant underlying record and its amendments. If a source is inaccessible, preserve the attempted locator and mark NOT REVIEWED. Do not use a confidence label to conceal unavailable source content. The report should state which uncertainties could change the recommendation and which merely limit descriptive detail.
