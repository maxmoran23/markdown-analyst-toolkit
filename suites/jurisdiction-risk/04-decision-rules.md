# Decision rules — Jurisdiction Risk and Exposure Review

## Category and exposure decision rules

| Condition | Required treatment | Not supported |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strategic-deficiency category supplied | Preserve exact category, issuer, date, and applicability | Treat as sanctions designation |
| Sanctions program question identified | Review relevant rule and transaction facts | Infer prohibition from country name |
| Corruption-related indicator supplied | Preserve method, vintage, uncertainty, and use limits | Assign individual culpability |
| Beneficiary geography missing | UNKNOWN bucket remains in total | Exclude from denominator |
| Several geographic roles per transfer | Separate role totals and deduplicated unions | Sum duplicated economic value |
| Mixed currencies | Separate totals or documented conversion | Add unlike units |
| Current authority unavailable | Legal conclusion NOT ASSESSED | Reuse a remembered country list |
| Customer has a country connection | Describe that specific connection | Automatically approve or reject |

None of the fictional category names is a real-world country assertion. J-A through J-D are invented codes, not anonymized actual jurisdictions. There is no hidden mapping. A user cannot recover a current list by treating these examples as substitutes for real countries. Their purpose is to expose denominator and applicability errors.

## Legal-scope gate

A legal applicability conclusion requires the relevant authority, effective date, jurisdictional nexus, party or activity covered, transaction facts, and any material authorization or exception question. If one is missing, preserve a defined unresolved question. Do not treat a source's public accessibility as evidence that it covers the contemplated service. A general license or exception, where relevant, requires its actual terms and conditions; a title alone is insufficient.

This suite does not calculate ownership-based sanctions status or decide designation matches. If the question depends on those issues, identify the required specialist method and keep the geographic analysis available as supporting context. Do not average an unresolved legal issue into an overall numerical risk score. Record the human decision owner and pending status without taking action.

## Severity, confidence, and proposed response

A HIGH finding can be a material unknown destination, a potentially relevant restriction question, or a source-date mismatch that prevents a conclusion. It does not mean a country or customer is illicit. MODERATE confidence fits a reasoned relevance inference with incomplete facts. HIGH confidence fits the narrow arithmetic that T03 plus T06 total $400,000.00, while legal applicability to either remains unresolved. Keep both statements together.

Proposed responses include mapping repair, source refresh, targeted customer-context research, and specialist legal-scope assessment. Assign a role and specific evidence request. Use “pending” for human disposition until someone actually records a decision. Do not claim that a transaction was held or released merely because the report recommends review. Do not notify external parties, update a screening list, or change a customer profile through this manual suite.

## Handling changes and contrary evidence

If a jurisdiction's context changes between two cutoffs, preserve both snapshots and recalculate the relevant exposure under a stated temporal rule. A change in a category does not retroactively erase previous conditions. Conversely, an old concern cannot be presented as current without current supporting evidence. Distinguish a genuine source change from a correction to the analyst's extraction.

When new evidence shows that the bank location was a routing intermediary rather than the beneficiary's jurisdiction, correct the role mapping and every affected metric. Do not leave the old country label in the customer profile because it appeared in an earlier report. Retain the original source and reason for correction. Where a public source rebuts an allegation, incorporate the rebuttal with procedural context rather than treating it as an inconvenient exception.
