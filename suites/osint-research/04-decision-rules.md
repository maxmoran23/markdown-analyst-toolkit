# OSINT Research — decision rules

## Claim assessment table

| Evidence condition | Assessment | Permitted conclusion | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Identity resolved; direct record supports precise claim | SUPPORTED | State the narrow fact and source scope | Confirm currency if used for a current decision |
| Relevant authoritative record conflicts with claim | CONTRADICTED | Describe the discrepancy without asserting intent | Seek explanation, correction, or later record |
| Several stories copy one press release | UNRESOLVED unless that reported statement is the question | Attribute the assertion to its origin | Seek independent measurement |
| Name similar but stable identifier different | IDENTITY UNRESOLVED or exclude as different subject | Do not transfer the event to the target | Resolve linkage before use |
| No result in documented searches | UNRESOLVED for broad absence claim | Say no matching result was located in named sources | Examine coverage and alternative identifiers |
| Record exists but material passage unavailable | UNRESOLVED | Describe access limitation | Obtain authorized full text |
| Historical status differs from current record | Separate dated claims | State each status with its own date | Avoid treating change as contradiction |

## Confidence and consequence

HIGH requires a well-resolved subject and strong direct support for the specific statement, with no unexplained material contradiction. MODERATE permits partial corroboration or a source that is authoritative but limited in scope. LOW applies to tentative secondary or inferential support. NOT ASSESSABLE applies when the relevant evidence or identity is missing. These labels are qualitative review aids; do not give them numerical probabilities.

Severity is based on decision consequence. A discrepancy in the license required for a contemplated activity may be HIGH; an uncorroborated staffing claim may be MEDIUM; a harmless naming inconsistency may be LOW. The same fact can have a different severity under a different intended use. Record that use. Never describe every unknown as HIGH merely because it is unknown.

## Alternatives and escalation

For a contradiction, consider reporting-date mismatch, definition mismatch, entity mismatch, source error, expired authorization, incomplete update, or an inaccurate original claim. Test these alternatives where evidence allows. Do not infer intentional deception from a discrepancy alone. If a likely error has a benign explanation but no corroboration, record that explanation as a hypothesis rather than clearing the issue.

Escalate to a human reviewer when the claim could materially affect a person's reputation, a relationship decision, public publication, or legal interpretation. Escalation means present the evidence, uncertainty, and proposed next step. It does not mean send an email, contact an authority, or publish the report. If the question requires a legal determination, identify the exact unresolved interpretive point and the jurisdiction rather than presenting a generalized answer as legal clearance.

## Bounded negative wording

Use: “No matching entry was located in the supplied register extract covering the stated period under the documented identifiers.” Do not use: “There are no proceedings,” “the subject is clean,” or “no risk exists.” Absence claims require evidence about the completeness of the collection process. If that process cannot be demonstrated, the defensible result is a bounded search statement.

## Decision record

Keep the analyst's recommendation, a reviewer's disposition, and any follow-up action distinct. A proposed citation may be accepted with qualifications, rejected, or deferred. Record who actually made the decision and when; leave the field PENDING otherwise. A completed research pack is not authority to approve a transaction, onboard an entity, or issue a public statement.
