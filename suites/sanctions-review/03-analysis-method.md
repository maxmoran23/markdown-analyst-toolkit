# Sanctions Review — analysis method

## 1. Establish authority, nexus, and time

Create a rule record before assigning a status: authority issuer, exact instrument or provision, program, affected persons/property/activity, effective date, source retrieval time, and known exceptions or authorizations requiring review. Distinguish the event date, screening date, ownership effective date, and decision date. A present list screen alone cannot establish whether a party was subject to a restriction on an earlier date.

Map the relevant parties and roles. A transaction can involve a contracting party, beneficial holder, intermediary, signatory, consignee, service provider, and payment recipient. Do not assume the named customer is the only relevant party, but also do not invent parties absent from the facts. Record unresolved roles and explain why they could matter under the identified authority.

For several jurisdictions, make separate scoped findings. An authority-specific result should state its jurisdiction in the same sentence as the conclusion. If a rule is unclear, identify the precise interpretive question and route it to the responsible human specialist. The assistant's confidence cannot replace legal authority.

## 2. Review the candidate identity

Preserve the complete current listing, not just a search-result label. Compare entity/person type, names and aliases, identifiers, locations, dates, and other available attributes. Document agreements, discrepancies, unknowns, and possible data errors. An exact name match is a useful lead but may be common; a stable identifier conflict can be decisive if both values are reliable and comparable.

Treat transliteration, reordered names, abbreviations, and former names as documented transformations. Keep originals. Do not invent aliases or infer nationality from a name. A missing date of birth or address neither confirms nor refutes identity. A third-party matching score is metadata about that tool's comparison, not a calibrated probability of identity or a legally prescribed threshold.

Classify the identity as SUPPORTED MATCH, SUPPORTED NONMATCH, or UNRESOLVED for human review. These are analytical recommendations based on the supplied evidence. Record the reason and source for any disqualifying attribute; a reviewer must be able to reconstruct why a candidate was excluded. If data are incomplete, retain the unresolved state instead of forcing a binary result.

## 3. Prepare a dated ownership graph

Use direct ownership edges with unique IDs. Each edge needs owner, owned entity, comparable ownership percentage, basis, effective period, and evidence. Normalize names to stable entity IDs only after identity resolution. Distinguish branches with unknown owners, unknown percentages, incompatible classes, and disputed dates.

Reconcile percentages per owned entity under the selected comparable basis and date. Do not combine a 2024 shareholder register with a 2026 acquisition announcement as though both describe the same moment. Do not add an upstream indirect interest to the intermediate holder's direct stake; that can count the same ownership twice. Unknown residual holdings stay visible.

If an edge is a range, retain lower and upper values in notes and analyze the range explicitly. If legally relevant rights cannot be represented by comparable percentages, do not force them into the illustration. Complex trusts, cross-holdings, control rights, contingent interests, disputed ownership, or mixed share classes require specialist interpretation of the actual authority.

## 4. Apply the scoped propagation worksheet

For the simple OFAC illustration, maintain a set of already established blocked holders and a separate set of entities under review. On each pass, total distinct direct ownership stakes held by already blocked holders in each entity. When the applicable threshold is met, record the inputs, pass number, and reason before adding that entity to the propagation set. Repeat until a pass adds no entity. This is a reproducibility procedure for a supported rule, not permission to decide real legal status without evidence.

Do not initialize a cycle by declaring its members blocked merely because they own each other. Start only from established seeds. Do not count both a parent's economic interest and its blocked subsidiary's full direct stake in the same target. The worksheet must show exactly which direct edges contribute to each total. Retain a pass-by-pass trace in the report notes so a reviewer can reproduce the sequence manually.

Where known blocked ownership is below the applicable threshold but unknown ownership could change the result, state INDETERMINATE. If known ownership already reaches the threshold, an unknown residual does not erase that mathematical result, although other legal and factual questions remain. Distinguish this sufficient-condition observation from a complete sanctions determination. Use the assumptions explicitly; do not generalize the method to other regimes.

## 5. Keep other restrictions separate

A result under one ownership rule does not dispose of control, signatory, geographic, government, sectoral, service, goods, financing, or other activity restrictions. Create a separate question for each nexus actually implicated by the facts. Do not use a generic country-risk label as a legal answer. The report must identify which questions were assessed and which remain NOT ASSESSED.

Similarly, a non-SDN entry does not automatically carry an SDN-style blocking consequence. Read the operative restriction and relevant transaction conditions. The existence of a license is not enough: the holder or general scope, activity, dates, conditions, and reporting requirements must fit the contemplated conduct. Treat claimed authorization as unverified until the relevant text and facts are reconciled.

## 6. Analyze change and historical property separately

When a party reports a divestment, preserve the prior and current ownership snapshots, agreement and completion dates, consideration if relevant and public, beneficial arrangements, and source limitations. A press release saying an interest will be sold is not evidence the transfer occurred. A post-designation change may raise different questions from an ordinary historical update.

Keep the status of newly contemplated activity separate from treatment of property already subject to a prior restriction. Do not overwrite a prior blocking or restriction history because the graph now shows a lower percentage. Route release or unblocking questions to the authorized decision process with the exact authority and property history.

## 7. Assemble the human review packet

For each subject, state identity assessment, applicable authority, relevant ownership result, other nexus, authorization status, unresolved evidence, and recommended review action. A reviewer should see the decisive facts without reconstructing them from a network diagram. Use diagrams only as aids; the edge table is authoritative.

The final report must distinguish analytical result, recommended next step, and actual human disposition. It must not claim a transaction was blocked, rejected, processed, reported, or cleared unless the user supplies a real authorized action record, and it must never perform those actions itself. If an urgent question is outside the supplied evidence, identify the missing item directly rather than offering false reassurance.
