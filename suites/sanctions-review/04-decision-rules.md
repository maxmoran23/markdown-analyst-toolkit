# Sanctions Review — decision rules

## Assessment matrix

| Condition | Analytical state | Permitted statement | Required next review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Name similarity only | Identity UNRESOLVED | Candidate match needs identifiers | Full entry and reliable subject attributes |
| Reliable incompatible unique identifiers | SUPPORTED NONMATCH, subject to review | Candidate appears different on named evidence | Document disqualifying comparison |
| Multiple reliable identifiers align | SUPPORTED MATCH, subject to review | Identity support is strong | Determine operative effect and authorization |
| Applicable ownership threshold met on supported edges | THRESHOLD MET | Scoped ownership condition met on supplied facts | Human sanctions disposition and other nexus |
| Known blocked interest below threshold; unknown residual could change it | INDETERMINATE | Available data do not resolve ownership condition | Missing owners/percentages and dates |
| Below threshold with complete stipulated comparable ownership | THRESHOLD NOT MET ON SUPPLIED FACTS | This ownership condition not met | Other rules and nexuses remain separate |
| Control evidence without relevant ownership threshold | Separate control question | Do not force control into ownership arithmetic | Apply actual jurisdiction/program authority |
| Claimed license without operative text | Authorization UNRESOLVED | Authorization not demonstrated | Exact terms, applicability, conditions, validity |
| Claimed divestment after prior restriction | Historical treatment UNRESOLVED | No automatic release inference | Property history and specialist determination |

## Severity and confidence

HIGH means an unresolved or supported issue could materially affect the permitted treatment of the activity. MEDIUM means a relevant evidence or process deficiency requires resolution but the supplied facts do not establish the most consequential outcome. LOW means a minor documentation issue without a demonstrated effect on the current question. These are prioritization categories, not regulatory labels.

Confidence follows the evidence for the exact proposition. It can be HIGH that a name-only candidate is unresolved, while confidence in the underlying identity is NOT ASSESSABLE. It can be HIGH that a supplied graph crosses a numerical condition, while confidence in the graph's accuracy is only MODERATE. Report both layers in the rationale so the arithmetic does not launder uncertain inputs into certainty.

## Alternative explanations to examine

A mismatch can arise from transliteration, old records, duplicate names, former entities, source error, or a truly different person. Ownership evidence can differ because of reporting dates, share-class treatment, nominee structures, incomplete filings, or disputed transactions. A purported license may apply to another affiliate, another activity, or another period. Test these possibilities against records rather than choosing the most favorable or most adverse explanation automatically.

A low name score must not override an exact reliable identifier. A high name score must not override a demonstrated different entity type or incompatible identifier without resolving the conflict. No universal numerical score is prescribed by this pack. If an organization supplies an authorized matching policy, apply it transparently and retain the underlying evidence comparison.

## Non-action boundary

The output is a review packet. It can recommend prompt review, additional evidence, correction of a false match, or specialist interpretation. It cannot itself authorize blocking, rejecting, releasing property, filing a report, processing a transaction, removing a restriction, or contacting an authority. Record actual decisions only when a human supplies them with date and rationale. Leave PENDING where no decision has been made.

## Wording rules

Use “the scoped ownership condition is met on the supplied fictional facts” for the example. For actual cases, state the exact authority and evidence limitations. Avoid “sanctioned globally,” “safe below 50%,” “cleared by no hit,” “all sanctions checked,” or “the AI approved.” A defensible sentence names the test, facts, date, and remaining questions.
