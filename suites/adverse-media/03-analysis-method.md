# Adverse Media — analysis method

## 1. Freeze the population and timeline

Create an input inventory before drawing conclusions. Separate source items, event candidates and planned searches. A source can discuss several events; several sources can discuss the same event. Never divide adverse article count by customer count and label the result a misconduct rate. Reconcile received records into processed, duplicate, excluded, unparsed, inaccessible and deferred categories. A syndicated article can be processed for lineage while counted as no new origin; ingestion duplicate and informational dependence are different concepts.

Place event, publication, retrieval and decision dates on separate timeline entries. Retain the evidence cutoff as a fixed point. Information received later can be used in a revised assessment with a new cutoff, but must not be silently inserted into an as-of reconstruction. Unknown dates remain unknown; the article's publication date is not a substitute for an unstated event date.

## 2. Resolve identity and role

For each source compare stable identifiers, jurisdiction, legal name, aliases, business description and relevant dates. State the decisive match or mismatch. Use MATCHED only where the evidence supports attribution at the necessary confidence; use UNRESOLVED when ambiguity remains and DIFFERENT_SUBJECT when there is affirmative contrary identity evidence. Exclude different-subject items from target event totals while preserving their rows as review history.

Establish the subject's role. An article about a fraud may name the subject as victim, witness or service provider. A company connected through an officer may have a distinct legal exposure. Explain the linkage actually supported and avoid writing an adverse description that generalizes beyond it. If the available identifiers are too weak to decide, prioritize resolving identity before requesting extensive conduct evidence.

## 3. Build event and proposition records

Assign one event ID to a distinct alleged episode or proceeding. Document the consolidation rationale: same parties, alleged conduct, time period, proceeding number and underlying facts. A later procedural update ordinarily changes the same event. A separate proceeding concerning different conduct may warrant another event even when the subject is identical. Preserve a cross-reference when two proceedings overlap so their amounts or counts are not double-counted.

Within each event distinguish the proposition that a source made an allegation from the proposition that the alleged conduct occurred. An observed complaint supports the first; the second requires its own evidence. Separate monetary allegations, amounts ordered and amounts paid. Do not sum these different categories into a single harm total. The fixture's monetary order is context and does not enter its event-count metrics.

## 4. Map source families and support direction

Trace each material statement upstream. Mark supports, contradicts, updates-status or context for the relevant proposition. A dismissal order can update procedural status without deciding the underlying facts. A denial conflicts with an allegation but does not establish its falsity. Two independent sources repeating the same prosecutorial notice remain one upstream origin for the accusation; their separate inspection of different records may provide additional support for other facts.

For each event count eligible adverse assertion origins, not total URLs. The illustrative count is the number of distinct upstream family IDs for sources actually supplying the adverse allegation. It does not score credibility, and one official origin can be more informative than many anonymous ones. Keep the family labels and rationale visible so reviewers can reproduce the count. Do not multiply confidence by a family count or convert it to a probability.

## 5. Determine current procedural meaning

Read the operative record and construct the supported sequence: allegation, inquiry, charge, adjudication, settlement, dismissal, appeal, reversal or another explicitly described state. These are labels for evidence, not a presumed inevitable path. Distinguish criminal, civil, regulatory and private proceedings. A settlement with no admission is not automatically an admission; a dismissal is not automatically a finding of factual innocence. Describe the precise issue decided and any limits in the document.

Where two records disagree, first test whether they address the same party, case, charge and date. A dismissal of one count does not dispose of every count. A pending appeal does not by itself vacate a decision. If the effect of a procedural instrument is unclear, state the document text and seek authorized legal interpretation without inventing a jurisdiction-independent rule. Preserve the latest supported status and a current-status gap when later records are unavailable.

## 6. Assess relevance and alternatives

Link the event to the stated decision through a specific risk mechanism: conduct, subject role, current relationship scope and the evidence's age and status. Avoid reputation by association. Explain whether the supported event raises a need for clarification, a source correction, a control inquiry or no additional adverse inference within scope. A low-confidence allegation may justify obtaining a primary record while remaining unsuitable as an established fact in the decision brief.

For each material concern state the strongest competing account and the evidence needed to distinguish it. Possibilities include mistaken identity, inaccurate summary, procedural reversal, repeated original reporting, an unrelated business dispute, an incident involving a victim rather than perpetrator, or a record outside the review period. Do not add an alternative merely to dismiss it without testing available evidence.

## 7. Reproduce metrics and preserve revision logic

Attributable events = distinct MATCHED event IDs. Unresolved attributable events = MATCHED events with unresolved allegation status at cutoff. Different-subject candidates remain in the total candidate count but outside attributable totals. Reviewed event-source eligibility = eligible event sources divided by all reviewed event sources; context-only identity records are outside both operands. Search completion = completed planned searches divided by all planned searches. None of these measures estimates how much adverse material exists in the world.

The example gives four candidates, three attributable events, one unresolved attributable allegation, seven eligible items among eight reviewed event-source items, and four completed searches of five planned. Display 7/8 as 87.5% and 4/5 as 80.0%, while keeping their different denominators explicit. A copied article can change the first denominator without changing the number of events or upstream allegation origins.

Finally compare the updated answer with the initial suspicion. If contrary evidence materially changes the interpretation, explain the reversal in the opening paragraph and update every dependent artifact. Preserve the prior version in the change record. Do not retain an unsupported adverse label to avoid appearing inconsistent.
