# Adverse Media — decision rules

## Gate sequence

| Gate | Sufficient result | If not satisfied |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Permitted scope | Clear question and authorized public inputs | Limit output to scoped background and missing inputs |
| Identity | Supported match to the relevant legal person | UNRESOLVED or DIFFERENT_SUBJECT; no target attribution |
| Role | Source identifies the subject's relevant conduct or status | Preserve context without transferring culpability |
| Source availability | Operative passage reviewed and located | Attribute narrowly or mark access limited |
| Lineage | Upstream assertion identified or uncertainty recorded | No assumed independence or inflated corroboration |
| Current status | Relevant updates checked within a bounded plan | State last supported status and coverage gap |
| Human disposition | Named authorized reviewer decision evidenced | Keep all recommendations proposed and draft |

These gates are toolkit conventions for disciplined analysis, not legal thresholds or prescribed screening rules. A failed gate blocks the affected statement, not necessarily every supported fact in the case. Explain which conclusion is prevented and the smallest evidence needed to revisit it.

## Interpretation by status

ALLEGATION_UNRESOLVED means the supplied information alleges conduct but does not establish it. Attribute the allegation, assess source limitations, preserve the response and recommend proportionate clarification if decision-relevant. Do not use definitive perpetrator labels. A repeated anonymous claim remains an anonymous claim unless distinct support is identified.

DISMISSED means a reviewed record supports that disposition for the specified matter. State whether the source identifies a procedural basis and whether it addresses facts. Do not describe the charge as pending after a clear dismissal; do not infer a broader exoneration beyond the record. If dismissal is merely reported, say who reports it and keep confidence matched to that evidence.

FINAL_ORDER means the reviewed operative order is final within the supplied record and cutoff. Describe its forum, subject, findings or agreed terms, and any known appeal or amendment. Do not relabel it a criminal conviction. The absence of an appeal from a supplied packet supports only that no appeal evidence was supplied; it does not establish an exhaustive docket search.

DIFFERENT_SUBJECT is an identity exclusion, not a disposition of the underlying proceeding. Retain the source and exclusion reason. UNRESOLVED identity means the target linkage is not established; a severe allegation cannot compensate for a weak match. When new identifiers arrive, reconsider identity independently from the event's substantive credibility.

## Severity and confidence

HIGH severity denotes a potentially consequential unresolved issue or material operative finding affecting the stated decision. MEDIUM denotes a relevant issue needing clarification or a source-quality defect capable of distorting the answer. LOW denotes bounded context, a supported exclusion or a corrected historical item with no identified unresolved gate in the supplied scope. These are illustrative analyst priorities, not a required institution-wide risk scale.

HIGH confidence requires direct, suitably scoped evidence and little ambiguity for the particular statement. MODERATE reflects partial corroboration or a material interpretive limit. LOW reflects weak attribution, a single unverified account or major missing evidence. NOT ASSESSABLE applies when the necessary identity or source basis is absent. A high-confidence observation that a charge was dismissed is compatible with no assessment of whether the original allegation was true.

Never combine severity and confidence into a single score that disguises what is unknown. State the claim first, then its evidence class and support. Where a paragraph mixes several claims, separate their assessments. The subject's economic importance, public visibility or unfamiliar jurisdiction cannot substitute for source-specific evidence.

## Response, correction and proportionate next steps

A supplied right-of-reply statement should be represented accurately and linked to the allegation it addresses. Test whether it provides verifiable facts, merely denies the claim, or addresses a different issue. A denial does not automatically clear the concern, and a missing response does not strengthen the allegation. Recommending that an authorized person obtain clarification does not mean the subject has already been contacted.

Escalate a material unresolved identity, operative-status ambiguity or decision-relevant contradiction to the appropriate human reviewer. The recommendation should specify the question, required source and consequence of each plausible answer. Do not recommend relationship termination solely from a headline count, copied coverage or unresolved namesake. The suite provides evidence assessment, not a universal acceptance or exit policy.

Preserve a correction even if it weakens the original concern. Reassess affected statements, remove unsupported present-tense allegations, and show what remains historically true. When an event falls outside scope, explain the exclusion rather than delete the record. A human may decide that an old event remains relevant for a stated reason; the analysis must make that reason visible instead of applying an unexplained age cutoff.
