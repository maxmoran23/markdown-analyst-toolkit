# Decision rules — Control Testing and Evidence Review

## Ordered disposition gates

Apply the gates in order. These are workpaper rules, not instructions to change a live process.

| Gate | Condition | Analytical treatment | Proposed human next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Objective or criterion missing | Unable to assess that dimension | Approve a criterion or restrict scope |
| 2 | Population frame unreconciled | Coverage unverified | Obtain source reconciliation |
| 3 | Design does not address stated risk | Design gap regardless of sample pass rate | Assess exposure and redesign |
| 4 | Evidenced execution deviation | FAIL for affected instance | Determine extent, cause, and action |
| 5 | Material execution evidence absent | UNABLE TO CONCLUDE | Recover evidence or accept limitation |
| 6 | All applicable attributes evidenced | PASS for that instance | Reviewer validates interpretation |
| 7 | Sample and design conclusions assembled | Draft conclusion with boundaries | Human disposition required |

## Severity is consequence based

HIGH describes an issue that may materially undermine the stated control objective, affect a critical population, or expose the process to an unmitigated failure mechanism. MEDIUM describes a substantive weakness with a narrower demonstrated impact or a material evidence gap. LOW describes a limited documentation or process improvement with no demonstrated material impairment within scope. Explain the judgment; do not map every missing field to the same severity.

Severity and confidence can move independently. A potentially severe failure supported only by incomplete evidence may be HIGH severity and LOW confidence. The recommendation should seek resolution without presenting the allegation as proven. The worked example uses illustrative severity assignments for fictional criteria. It is not an institution's issue-rating policy.

## Conclusion alternatives

| Evidence pattern | Supported wording | Wording to reject |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design suitable, selected instances pass | No deviations observed in the selected records, with scope stated | Entire program effective |
| Judgmental sample contains failures | Defined deviations observed in selected records | Estimated population error rate |
| One selected record lacks evidence | Operation could not be assessed for that record | Control did not occur, without more evidence |
| Procedure permits self-approval | Design lacks the stated separation safeguard | Reviewer misconduct |
| Owner reports an action complete | Completion reported; evidence pending | Finding closed |
| Compensating control claimed | Potential mitigation awaiting testing | Risk eliminated |

Choose additional testing when a failure mechanism is uncertain or could be widespread. Choose a focused design recommendation when the procedure itself is incapable of satisfying the objective. Choose a restricted conclusion when evidence cannot be recovered within scope. A timeline pressure does not change evidence quality; record the limitation and the decision owner.

## Sampling changes and exclusions

A replacement record never erases the original missing or unreadable record. A selected event found to be outside the predefined population may be excluded with a precise reason, provided the rule is applied consistently to every record. A failed event does not become out of scope merely because it was inconvenient, manually processed, or handled by a different team. If the definition changes materially, create a revised population version and explain how results differ.

Approval of a larger sample is not automatic approval of the conclusion. A larger judgmental sample can deepen issue understanding while still lacking probability-based inference. If the analyst wants a population estimate, require a suitable selection design and inference method rather than relabeling the existing sample.

## Human decisions that remain open

The authorized reviewer decides whether the objective and criteria are appropriate, whether independence is adequate, whether scope expansion is needed, whether an alternative control mitigates the issue, and whether remediation evidence supports closure. The assistant may draft an issue, action plan, or proposed conclusion. It does not certify effectiveness, close findings, make disciplinary judgments, or attest to regulatory compliance. Keep each proposed decision and its evidence visible in the Actions and QA sheets.
