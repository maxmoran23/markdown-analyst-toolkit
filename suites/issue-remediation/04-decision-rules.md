# Decision rules — Issue Remediation and Closure Review

## Ordered closure gates

These are author-defined analytical gates. Apply the actual approved closure standard when supplied, preserve its source, and explain any departure from this illustration.

| Gate | Evidence required | Failure treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scope and impact | Original condition and affected extent addressed | Keep scope restriction and required investigation |
| Causal mechanism | Supported explanation or explicit justified limit | Require further cause analysis or conditional plan |
| Implementation | Inspected change and adoption evidence | Completion remains reported or not evidenced |
| Effectiveness | Fresh relevant retest satisfying the criterion | Failed or incomplete validation blocks readiness |
| Recurrence | Prior episodes and new observations reconciled | Reopen or expand analysis where supported |
| Residual risk | Current authorized disposition where relevant | Escalate expired, absent, or out-of-scope acceptance |
| Independent approval | Actual authorized review and recorded decision | Closure remains pending |

A high completion percentage cannot override a failed gate. A low number of open tasks cannot establish low residual risk. Gates concern different claims; a signed policy does not substitute for post-change results, and a successful retest does not establish an expired acceptance has been renewed.

## Action and issue states

| Evidence pattern | Action treatment | Issue treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner says complete, no supporting artifact | Not evidenced | Remains open |
| Implementation artifact satisfies its criterion | Verified implementation | Await dependent effectiveness evidence |
| Fresh retest demonstrates a failure | Failed validation | Closure blocked; investigate mechanism |
| Fresh retest includes an unavailable record | Unresolved validation | Closure blocked by evidence limit |
| All technical gates evidenced, final decision absent | Actions may be Verified | Closure review pending |
| Acceptance expires without renewal | Acceptance not current | Acceptance expired; active exposure remains |
| New same-mechanism event after closure | Preserve historical action results | Reopened after human status decision is evidenced |

Do not infer closure from a file named “closure pack.” Do not infer approval from a meeting invitation, silence, or the passage of a deadline. Record proposed decisions as proposals. If the complete authorized register establishes a reopening, it may be recorded as an observed historical status; otherwise recommend reopening for review.

## Timeliness rules

Current issue overdue days use the reference date and approved current due date while no valid closure exists. Current action overdue days use the same logic while its acceptance criterion remains unmet. Completed action lateness is historical and remains visible separately. Under the fixture's date convention, a due date equal to the reference date is not yet overdue; a one-day difference produces one overdue day.

An approved extension changes the operative due date from its valid effective point. Keep the original due date and any earlier breach. A proposed extension has no effect. An undocumented extension receives a gap, not a new date. A closed issue lacking evidence of authorized closure cannot be treated as conclusively closed merely to lower the overdue count.

The fixture's I03 is technically ready but two days overdue because its issue due date was Oct 07 and closure approval is absent at Oct 09. A05 is not currently overdue because its completion criterion was evidenced Sep 26. These facts are consistent: action completion and issue closure have separate clocks.

## Cause and severity decisions

Choose a causal redesign when observed recurrence shows the current action does not interrupt the mechanism. Choose evidence recovery when the remaining uncertainty is a missing artifact that could distinguish pass from fail. Choose broader scope when the mechanism plausibly affects unexamined channels and the submitted scope cannot exclude them. Each recommendation should identify the observation that would change it.

Severity follows the consequence and exposure of the issue, not the number of late days or the number of attached documents. The fixture labels I01 and I04 HIGH, I02 and I03 MEDIUM as illustrative judgments. Confidence describes the evidence for the claim, not the importance of addressing it. A severe unresolved exposure can require urgent human attention while its causal confidence remains limited.

## Risk acceptance and final authority

Accepting a residual risk is a separate human governance decision with an authority basis and duration. It is not an alternate spelling of successful remediation. An acceptance may permit a defined temporary operating posture under applicable policy; it does not amend a legal obligation or prove a control effective. Renewal needs an actual new decision, not a copied expiry date.

Final disposition must name the deciding role and preserve the reviewed evidence. The assistant can identify readiness, blockers, alternatives, and proposed actions. It cannot issue an institutional certification, approve its own review, send operational instructions, or manufacture an independent reviewer. A reviewer who implemented the action may provide evidence but does not automatically satisfy an independent closure gate.
