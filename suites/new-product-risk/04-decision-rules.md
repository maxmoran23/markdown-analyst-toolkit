# Decision rules — New Product Risk and Launch Readiness

## Ordered recommendation gates

| Gate | Condition | Analytical recommendation | Required human action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Product purpose or primary flows unknown | Unable to assess | Resolve scope and responsibility |
| 2 | Required legal or activity authority unresolved | Restrict recommendation | Obtain appropriate determination |
| 3 | Critical launch condition not satisfied | Evidence does not support launch yet | Resolve or formally reconsider condition |
| 4 | Claimed compensating control untested | Do not count mitigation as established | Inspect design and execution evidence |
| 5 | Only legitimate post-launch items remain | Conditional proposal may be described | Approve scope, owner, deadline, monitoring |
| 6 | All required evidence supports criteria | Eligible for human launch consideration | Authorized decision and conditions |

Criticality is a review classification that must be explained. It is not synonymous with a statutory requirement. In the worked example, five of six conditions are critical because the fictional approval record says so; no condition is reclassified after outcomes are known.

## Status decision table

| Evidence pattern | Status | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All criteria supported for correct version and timing | SATISFIED | Complete flow map accepted for fixture |
| Some elements supported; one required element incomplete | PARTIAL | Reconciliation detects but does not resolve delayed item |
| Evidence shows required work absent or test failed | NOT SATISFIED | Rehearsal has not been performed |
| Completion asserted with no inspectable supporting artifact | NOT VERIFIABLE | Owner says monitoring test passed |
| Valid documented scope exclusion approved by human | NOT APPLICABLE | Removed feature with approved condition change |

Do not infer that NOT VERIFIABLE means the control definitely did not exist. Do not infer that PARTIAL is almost approved. The next action should identify exactly what evidence would change the status.

## Alternatives and trade-offs

Delay the launch when essential prerequisites remain unsupported. Narrow the product only when the removed flows and associated risks are demonstrably outside the revised scope and the restriction can be enforced. Propose a staged pilot when its controls and exit behavior are verified and the remaining conditions legitimately permit staging. Decline to recommend the product when the intended operating model cannot address a material risk within the approved boundaries.

A compensating measure must address the same failure mechanism at the needed time and scale. Manual review can be meaningful at a small volume if staff, data, escalation, and evidence are available; it is not automatically equivalent to an unavailable automated control. Compare workload, independence, coverage, and error paths. A document saying “manual fallback available” is a claim to test.

## Severity and decision restraint

HIGH covers an unresolved material control dependency, a critical failed or unverified launch condition, or a credible failure that could undermine the product's objective. MEDIUM covers a substantive but bounded supporting gap. LOW covers a limited improvement or documentation issue with explained consequence. Use confidence for the evidence, not for enthusiasm about the product.

The assistant may recommend a course and draft decision language. It cannot accept residual risk, approve a pilot, waive an agreed condition, change limits, enable a feature, or authorize funds movement. Keep the human decision pending and distinguish evidence acceptance from launch approval. A committee-ready document is not a committee decision.
