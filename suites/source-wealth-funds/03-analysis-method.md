# Analysis method — Source of Wealth and Funds Review

## 1. Establish two scopes and a chronological spine

Write a wealth question and a funds question separately. The fixture's wealth question concerns the subject's net assets from Jan 01 to Sep 30, 2025. The funding question concerns an Oct 01, 2025 proposal. A later proposed transfer cannot be included as a completed earlier receipt. If settlement occurred after the cutoff, show a subsequent-event scenario instead of rewriting historical cash. Dates matter even when total amounts happen to reconcile.

Create a chronological spine of acquisition, entitlement, income recognition, disposal, settlement, taxation, valuation, and transfer events. Identify which events are effective and which are proposed. A legal entitlement date can precede receipt, and market value can change before disposal. Preserve those distinctions so a narrative does not turn expected wealth into immediately available funds. Where a date is uncertain, show the affected conclusion as conditional.

## 2. Normalize ownership and opening wealth

Define net wealth as the selected owned assets less associated liabilities at the measurement date, subject to the scope and valuation assumptions. Do not call this a comprehensive verified net-worth assessment if omitted assets or debts are unknown. The fixture deliberately assumes a complete fictional opening set: cash $400,000.00, existing securities $600,000.00, property $500,000.00, and debt $200,000.00. Net opening wealth is $1,300,000.00.

Separate economic ownership from management authority. A person who signs on an account does not necessarily own its assets. A controlling shareholder does not automatically own all company cash personally. Joint property requires a supported share and liability treatment. If the source says “family assets,” allocate nothing to the subject until entitlement is supplied. A source's prestige does not replace the missing ownership fact.

## 3. Build a bridge with stock, flow, and valuation categories

Classify events before summing: opening stock, net income, noncash acquisition, disposal gain or loss, expense, unrealized revaluation, liability change, and internal transfer. The bridge adds changes in net wealth to opening net wealth. A cash ledger separately explains movements in liquidity. Many events affect one differently from the other. An inherited security increases owned assets without producing cash; its later sale produces cash while removing the asset's carrying amount.

The fixed WealthBridge table includes opening components and period changes so summing its usd_wealth_change column yields closing net wealth in the fixture. Its cash_effect_usd column similarly includes opening cash and subsequent cash changes. Label this convention explicitly: an opening row is not income during the period. Do not compare the sum of all original_amount values with net wealth because those values use different currencies and economic meanings.

## 4. Reconcile gross income to net amounts

For each income event, bridge gross entitlement to deductions and net receipt. The fictional salary is gross $300,000.00 less paid tax $90,000.00, yielding $210,000.00 cash and wealth increase. The tax is already reflected in the net contribution and must not be deducted again. This is arithmetic using invented tax inputs, not an inferred tax rate applicable to an actual person.

The dividend is EUR100,000.00 gross. At the supplied fictional rate of 1.20 USD per EUR, gross translated value is $120,000.00. A fictional $30,000.00 paid tax leaves $90,000.00. The exercise assumes no other fees and no unrecognized exchange difference. In a real permitted public-data analysis, separate actual conversion proceeds from a reporting translation: a translated balance does not establish the cash received at that rate.

## 5. Treat inherited assets and their sale as one connected chain

The subject inherits 4,000 fictional security units at $100.00 each: $400,000.00 at transfer. Later 2,000 units are sold for $125.00 each: gross $250,000.00. The carrying amount of those sold units is $200,000.00. Fees of $10,000.00 and paid fictional tax of $20,000.00 produce net cash of $220,000.00. Remaining inherited holdings are 2,000 units at the unchanged fixture carrying value of $100.00, or $200,000.00.

Incremental net wealth on disposal equals gross proceeds $250,000.00 minus carrying amount $200,000.00 minus fees $10,000.00 minus tax $20,000.00 = $20,000.00. Net cash increases $220,000.00 while inherited securities decline $200,000.00. The combined net change is $20,000.00. Adding the full $220,000.00 to the wealth bridge while leaving the inherited assets untouched would overstate wealth by $200,000.00.

The carrying value here is an exercise measurement basis, not a statement of legal tax basis or accounting policy. Taxable gain can use different rules; the fixture supplies tax paid independently rather than calculating tax from its valuation bridge. Do not use this method to advise on tax liability, inheritance law, or basis elections.

## 6. Separate cash availability from appreciation

Property appreciation of $100,000.00 and existing-security appreciation of $60,000.00 raise the closing wealth scenario by $160,000.00. They create zero cash in the supplied period. A sale, borrowing, or distribution might generate future liquidity, but none is assumed. Do not use market value as a source for a particular transfer without a supported conversion or financing event.

Period living expenses reduce cash and wealth by $120,000.00. Debt stays at $200,000.00; the fixture includes no repayment or new borrowing. Closing cash is opening $400,000.00 plus salary $210,000.00 plus sale $220,000.00 plus dividend $90,000.00 minus living expenses $120,000.00 = $800,000.00. This balance says cash is available within the fictional scenario, not that every possible transfer from it has a proved lawful origin.

## 7. Reconcile closing assets independently

Rebuild the closing balance sheet from quantities and asset categories rather than only trusting the event sum. Cash is $800,000.00, existing securities $660,000.00, remaining inherited securities $200,000.00, and property $600,000.00. Gross assets total $2,260,000.00. Deduct unchanged debt $200,000.00 for net wealth $2,060,000.00. Compare that with opening $1,300,000.00 plus net movement $760,000.00.

An unexplained difference needs a reconciliation item, not a forced plug labelled “other income.” Investigate date mismatch, fees, omitted expenses, duplicated sale proceeds, ownership proportions, valuation changes, and exchange effects. If no explanation is available, report the gap and withhold claims of completeness. A match can inherit shared unsupported assumptions; inspect the source chain.

## 8. Build a separate particular-funds allocation

List every proposed source of the $600,000.00 transfer and its supporting net amount. The fixture allocates $180,000.00 of net salary, $90,000.00 of net dividend, $220,000.00 of net inherited-asset sale proceeds, $50,000.00 of opening cash, and $60,000.00 from an unrelated third party. These sum to $600,000.00. The first four have fictional document support for amount and entitlement; the last lacks economic-origin and entitlement evidence.

Supported allocation totals $540,000.00 and unsupported allocation $60,000.00, giving 90.0% and 10.0%. These are evidence-allocation shares, not probabilities that money is lawful or illicit. The allocation is a proposed narrative backed by fixture records, not tracing identifiable currency units through a commingled account. Avoid implying that a salary dollar remained untouched while other cash funded living expenses unless the record supports that flow.

## 9. Reconcile duplicate documents and residual capacity

Two documents can describe one payment. Funding row F06 is duplicate advice for the same sale economic event SALE01 as F03. Retain the document in evidence but allocate zero additional dollars. Compare document identifiers with economic-event identifiers before summing. A sale agreement, broker confirmation, and bank credit can corroborate different links in a single chain; they are not three sources of money.

Check allocation against each net available amount, considering prior use where known. Salary allocation $180,000.00 is below its net $210,000.00, leaving $30,000.00 unallocated by that proposed label. The global cash balance remains the decisive feasibility check after all expenses; per-source residuals are not automatically distinct segregated balances. Supported own-cash allocation $540,000.00 is below closing cash $800,000.00, leaving $260,000.00 before any later events. The proposed third-party $60,000.00 is excluded from personal wealth because it has not been received or established as owned.

## 10. Challenge explanation and write separate conclusions

Test an alternative funding proposal using only existing owned cash. If the subject proposes replacing the unexplained $60,000.00 with documented opening cash, the total might remain feasible, but that is a revised proposal requiring its own evidence and disposition. Do not silently rewrite the supplied source schedule to remove an uncomfortable gap. Likewise, a plausible inheritance narrative does not authenticate the estate transfer or prove that the assets were free of restrictions.

Write one conclusion on the wealth scenario and another on the particular funds. Identify source confidence, missing links, and what evidence would change each conclusion. Propose targeted corroboration with a reviewer role and dependency. Do not approve funds, make a tax determination, allege wrongdoing, or report a human decision that has not been made. The useful result is a transparent bridge and a precise account of what remains unexplained.
