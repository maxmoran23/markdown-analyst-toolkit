# Decision rules — Regulatory Change and Obligation Mapping

## Status and action matrix

| Instrument state | Permitted analytical action | Deadline treatment | Human decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PROPOSED | Track, analyze scenarios, identify potential dependencies | Record comment date separately; no confirmed compliance date | Whether to plan or respond |
| FINAL_NOT_EFFECTIVE | Map verified requirements and transition steps | Preserve effective and compliance dates separately | Applicability and implementation plan |
| EFFECTIVE | Assess mapped requirements and evidence | Use provision-specific verified dates | Sufficiency and consequence assessment |
| GUIDANCE | Assess relevance and distinguish expectations from law | Do not invent a binding deadline | Applicability and proportionate response |
| WITHDRAWN | Preserve history and dependent plans | Remove from current-obligation totals | Disposition of preparatory work |
| STATUS_UNVERIFIED | Research and log uncertainty | Keep claimed date outside confirmed date fields | Interim response and source escalation |

These states simplify a register; they do not replace legal classification. An instrument may have mixed provision statuses or conditions. Add an explicit explanation rather than forcing a misleading single state. Do not treat every document called guidance as having identical legal significance across jurisdictions.

## Applicability gate

A requirement is not mapped as a confirmed current obligation unless primary status is verified, the operative provision is identified, applicable scope facts are supported, and timing is understood. If one element is unknown, retain the item with Conditional or Unresolved applicability and an action to resolve it. Unknown is not Out of scope.

A regulator's example, enforcement action against another party, industry recommendation, and proposed rule can inform analysis, but each has a different evidentiary role. Do not convert them into a universally binding control requirement without a specific applicable authority. The requirement summary should identify the source of the obligation, while the recommendation may propose a stronger control as a discretionary option.

## Readiness gate

| Readiness state | Evidence meaning | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BUILD | Implementation remains in development | Readiness or operation |
| EVIDENCE GAP | Required support not available | Proof that the required act never occurred |
| VERIFIED FOR FIXTURE | Fictional example evidence meets fictional criterion | Real-world compliance |
| SCENARIO ONLY | Potential action tied to proposal | Current obligation |
| REFERENCE ONLY | Context or guidance considered | Binding requirement |

For real supplied reviews, use a clearly named evidence-supported state appropriate to the actual scope; never reuse VERIFIED FOR FIXTURE for real operations. If an owner reports completion, preserve that as Reported and distinguish it from verified evidence. A human reviewer accepts or rejects readiness with conditions.

## Priority and alternatives

HIGH prioritizes a material in-scope readiness gap, significant unresolved applicability with a near-term consequence, or an urgent primary-source verification need. MEDIUM prioritizes planned work or substantive interpretation that requires attention but lacks the same immediate evidenced consequence. LOW is used for contextual, withdrawn, or low-impact items within this scope. These are illustrative workflow priorities, not legal materiality ratings.

For an uncertain provision, alternatives include obtaining legal interpretation, gathering a missing scope fact, implementing a reversible preparatory step, or waiting for a final text while monitoring the defined source. Each alternative states cost, reversibility, evidence dependency, and risk of acting too early or too late. The assistant does not choose a binding institutional legal interpretation, submit comments, notify a regulator, accept an exception, or approve a policy.

If the analyst asks for a simplified dashboard, preserve separate status and readiness fields. A single red/green compliance badge obscures the difference between a proposal, a future requirement, an evidence gap, and a confirmed implementation issue. Use explicit text and counts instead.
